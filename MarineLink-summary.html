CONCEPT, BRAND & VISUAL DESIGN

Marine Link

Most ocean content is informative. Marine Link is immersive.

An exploration-driven ocean experience that turns a scroll into a deep-sea descent for pre-K and early elementary kids.

Role Lead Designer: concept, brand & visual design Team 2 designers Platform Web Duration 2 weeks
01 · THE IDEA

Curiosity first, not a quiz

Most ocean content treats learning like a test, with dry facts and quizzes, and most websites are too hard for kids who can't read yet. Marine Link works like the ocean itself: kids sink deeper and discover one creature at a time.

5
ocean zones, from the sunlit surface to the hadal trenches
35
creatures, 7 per zone, so progress is a number a child can count toward
23
core components documented in the design harness

03 · THE SOLUTION

Scroll deeper, discover, collect

Depth is the navigation, with no tabs or menus. Every zone repeats the same three cues (name, depth, and an audio and color shift), so the pattern reads before a child can read the words.

CREATURE CARDS

Tap for a kawaii illustration and a spoken fact, then flip for a real photo.

CREATURE MAP

All 35 creatures by zone, with a "you are here" badge.

QUIZ

Opens from a trophy, not a "?". Picture answers, and confetti for a correct pick.

AUDIO

Voice-read facts, a tap-to-talk search, and a soundscape for each zone.

Each zone has its own palette, from bright surface light down to bioluminescent trench accents, so a pre-reader can tell "I'm somewhere new" from color alone.

04 · WHAT WE FIXED

The audit caught what the moodboard didn't

  • Red/green status on a dark backgroundFailed for colorblind users. Now icon-based status with clear labels.
  • Progress bar filled gold at 0/35Looked complete before any progress. Now a muted track that fills as creatures are found.
  • Filter tabs that didn't filter"Explored" still showed every creature. The selected tab now hides or dims the rest.
05 · THE DESIGN HARNESS

The fix was documentation, not more prompting

Claude's first build was rough: placeholder art, overlapping icons and colors that drifted between screens. I audited the shipped prototype and wrote a design harness: design tokens, 23 components with their states, a motion spec and a voice guide, plus a strict rule that nothing new gets invented without approval.

New screens then matched the shipped app on the first pass, with no more rounds of manual correction.
06 · REFLECTION

Prompting isn't a shortcut around design skill

The build proves that depth-as-scroll and a countable creature loop make exploring feel different from reading. It hasn't yet proven that exploring for fun and actually learning are the same thing. Next: a second layer of content for each creature, the accessibility fixes we've already identified, and testing with real kids in the target age group.
